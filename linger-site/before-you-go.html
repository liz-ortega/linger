Before you go

The things nobody told me to think about.

It's easy to say "I'll be okay" and figure it out later. Then the time comes, and you'd rather be out enjoying it than scrambling. This is what I wish I'd set up before my semester in Copenhagen.

Take care of yourself

Body

Make a sick plan

Know what works for you when you catch a cold or whatever you usually get easily. For me, tea and honey are my go-to, so step one in a new city was finding where to buy them.

  • Pack your usual cold and allergy basics
  • Bring prescriptions in your carry-on, in original packaging, and check that country's medicine rules
  • Find the nearest pharmacy and clinic in your first week
Mind

Set up support before you need it

Get a checkup and talk to a professional before you leave. Make an action plan together for the hard days, so you can feel supported and still enjoy it.

  • Ask whether your therapist or campus counseling can keep seeing you online from abroad
  • Find out what your program and your insurance offer there
  • Write down who you'd call, and save the numbers in your phone

Homesickness and culture shock are normal, and often hit a few weeks in. Your program office and your home campus counseling center can still help while you're abroad. In a crisis, call your local emergency number (112 across the EU). In the US, call or text 988.

Know your why

Goals

Decide what you want to get out of it

A semester goes by fast. If you know what you're there for, you'll spend your time on what matters. For me it was three things: meeting new people, learning some of the language, and getting academic and professional experience I couldn't get anywhere else. So I did an internship and research while I was there. Write down your two or three, and check back on them halfway through.

Your people

Friends

Find your people

Just like at home, not everyone will be your cup of tea, and not every group will feel right. That's okay. Keep showing up, stay true to who you are, and you'll find the people who make you feel good.

Home

Set call times early

Before you leave, agree on roughly when you're free and when your family is. A weekly rhythm beats guessing across time zones. The clock below helps.

Keepsakes

Find your way to remember

For me it was decorating and curating little photo posts. It kept my family in the loop and kept every memory safe in one place. Find the version that's yours.

The practical stuff

Plugs

Get the right adapters

Check the plug type for every country you'll visit, not just where you live. Bring a couple of adapters and a power strip so one adapter charges everything.

Money

Build a cushion

A side gig before you go, or a few scholarship applications, gives you room to say yes to trips instead of stressing over every euro.

Language

Learn a little first

Even a few weeks of Duolingo before you land makes ordering coffee and asking directions less scary, and helps a new place feel like yours faster.
